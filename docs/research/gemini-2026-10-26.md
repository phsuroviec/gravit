 1a. Shared applied_gravity(body, t) and Area3D Overrides 
 In Godot 4.3, PhysicsBody3D (inherited by both CharacterBody3D and RigidBody3D) provides 
 Vector3 get_gravity() const, which returns the gravity vector computed from all Area3D 
 overrides and global World3D settings affecting that body  16  . However, relying solely on Godot's 
 internal Area3D broadphase overlap requires waiting for a physics step after node instantiation 
 or teleportation, which complicates deterministic headless unit tests  6  . By maintaining an explicit 
 FoldScope (Area3D) hierarchy and querying FoldScope.applied_gravity(body, t, scopes) directly 
 while setting RigidBody3D.gravity_scale = 0.0 and Area3D.gravity_space_override = 
 Area3D.SPACE_OVERRIDE_DISABLED (0), both CharacterBody3D and RigidBody3D share the 
 exact same pure function without double-applying built-in gravity  6  . If you prefer to let Godot's 
 physics server compute get_gravity() natively, set each fold's Area3D.gravity_space_override = 
 Area3D.SPACE_OVERRIDE_REPLACE (enum value 3, which replaces default gravity and ignores 
 lower-priority areas) and bind area.priority = get_depth()  6  .

 1c. Carrying, Placing, and Breaking a Fold Containing Physics Objects 
 Official Godot 4.3 RigidBody3D documentation explicitly warns against keeping an active 
 RigidBody3D as a child of a moving node:  "Changing  the 3D transform or linear_velocity of a 
 RigidBody3D very often may lead to some unpredictable behaviors. This also happens when a 
 RigidBody3D is the descendant of a constantly moving node, like another RigidBody3D, as that 
 will cause its global transform to be set whenever its ancestor moves."  2  . Multiple Godot 4 issues 
 confirm that parenting an un-frozen RigidBody3D under a moving parent node causes it to 
 freeze in place or desynchronize from its collision shape  4  . 
 Therefore, do  not  keep active physics objects as scene-tree  children of the fold during normal 
 simulation  2  . Instead, follow a three-state reparenting  protocol: 
 1.  While placed in the room:  Keep all RigidBody3D objects  parented directly to RoomRoot 
 and track their scope membership in fold.tracked_bodies  6  . 
 2.  On pickup (pickup_fold):  Freeze every RigidBody3D  currently inside the fold 
 (body.freeze_mode = RigidBody3D.FREEZE_MODE_KINEMATIC, body.freeze = true) and 
 call body.reparent(fold, true) (keep_global_transform = true) so they move rigidly with 
 the carried fold box in front of the player camera  5  . 
 3.  On placement (place_fold) or break (break_fold):  Iterate  over the contained bodies, 
 call body.reparent(room_root, true), call body.reset_physics_interpolation(), and set 
 body.freeze = false  19  . When breaking a fold, reassign  every contained body and child 
 FoldScope to fold.parent_scope before removing the broken fold  1  .

 This structure directly eliminates the three classic delayed-queue failure modes in Godot 4.3. 
 First, regarding  paused physics  , SceneTree.create_timer()  defaults to process_always = true, 
 which continues ticking while SceneTree.paused == true and attempts to fire impulses while 3D 
 physics processing is halted  8  ; accumulating sim_time  += delta inside a 
 PROCESS_MODE_PAUSABLE node's _physics_process ensures the timer halts whenever the 
 scene tree pauses  8  . Second, regarding  freed bodies  ,  Godot 4 Object variables do not 
 automatically become null when freed, so checking body != null is insufficient and causes 
 runtime errors  20  ; calling @GlobalScope.is_instance_valid(item.body)  alongside not 
 item.body.is_queued_for_deletion() prevents dangling-reference crashes when a room resets  31  . 
 Third, regarding  double-application  , iterating backward  through _queue, setting item.fired = 
 true, and removing the entry prior to calling apply_central_impulse(item.impulse) guarantees 
 single execution even if an impulse callback triggers another scene event  7  .

 Recommendation:  Structure your three-room course around  a strict  One-Idea-Per-Room

 to open a specific  curiosity gap  , and guarantees  zero-cost  failure  (no lethal hazards, no

 Psychological Foundations of Fair, Teachable Puzzle Rooms 
 In  The Gamer's Brain  (2017) and her GDC onboarding  framework, Celia Hodent explains that 
 human working memory is severely capacity-limited and suffers from  encoding deficits  when 
 overloaded with simultaneous mechanics or text manuals  52  .  Hodent divides onboarding into 
 Usability  —removing friction via clear physical, cognitive,  and sensory affordances where  form 
 follows function  —and  Engage-ability  , which relies  on Self-Determination Theory (Deci & Ryan, 
 1985) to build intrinsic motivation through  competence  and  autonomy  52  . Teaching one rule at 
 a time and letting the player execute it immediately without HUD walls of text builds an early 
 sense of mastery  52  . 
 Once basic competence is established, engagement is driven by George Loewenstein's 
 Information Gap Theory of Curiosity  (  Psychological  Bulletin  , 1994), which defines curiosity as 
 a state of cognitively induced deprivation triggered when attention focuses on a narrow, 
 specific gap between what one knows and wants to know  56  .  Complete ignorance produces 
 little curiosity; the designer must "prime the pump" with partial knowledge so the player can 
 frame a concrete question  51  . Mark Brown (  Game Maker's  Toolkit  ) and Jonathan Blow (  Braid  ,  The 

<!-- page 19 -->
 Witness  ) operationalize this in puzzle design through the sequence of  Assumption → Catch 
 → Revelation  : the room invites an obvious assumption  based on the rule just learned, presents 
 a logical contradiction (the  Catch  ) where the naive  attempt fails by a clear margin, and rewards 
 the player with a  Revelation  when they recombine the  rule in a new way to close the curiosity 
 gap  53  . 
 Finally, Valve's  Portal  and  Portal 2  developer commentaries  demonstrate how  predictable 
 failure cost  and  visual telegraphing  keep experimentation  frustration-free  37  . In  Portal  Test 
 Chamber 01, Valve ensured closing portals never crushed or killed the player, pushing them 
 safely out so they trusted the mechanic  37  . In Chamber  04, Valve added a glass barrier to block 
 a backdoor solution (shooting a portal through the door while standing on the button) and 
 used a warm ceiling spotlight against cool chamber lighting to draw the player's eye directly to 
 the cube  37  . In  Portal 2  , airlock doors locked behind  players between chambers so they never 
 mistook prior rooms as part of the current puzzle  63  .
